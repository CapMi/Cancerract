1.⁠ ⁠Melanoma
Often looks like a new or changing mole
May be asymmetric
Uneven/jagged border
Multiple colors (brown, black, red, blue, etc.)
Changes in size, shape, or color are particularly important
Remember ABCDE: Asymmetry, Border, Color, Diameter, Evolving.

2.⁠ ⁠Pigmented Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)
Can look surprisingly similar to a brown or black mole
May be shiny, raised, or pearly
Can have several colors
May bleed, crust, or repeatedly form a sore
BCC is the most common type of skin cancer.

3.⁠ ⁠Pigmented Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)
Can appear as a brown/dark raised spot
Often feels rough, scaly, thick, or crusty
May grow or bleed
Can sometimes resemble an age spot or mole.

4.⁠ ⁠Merkel Cell Carcinoma
Usually not a typical brown mole
Often appears as a rapidly growing firm red, pink, or purple bump
Can be shiny and dome-shaped
It is much less common than BCC, SCC, and melanoma.

5.⁠ ⁠Dermatofibrosarcoma Protuberans (DFSP)
A rare skin cancer
Often starts as a firm, relatively flat or slightly raised patch
Can gradually develop into nodules/lumps
It can be mistaken for other benign skin growths.
